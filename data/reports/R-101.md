# Global Smart Water Meter Market 2025

Cognitive Market Research (CMR). Published 10 March 2025. Sector: Utilities.

This report sizes the global smart water meter market and forecasts its development over the period 2025 to 2030. It is intended for utility executives, infrastructure investors and technology vendors planning metering programmes. Base year for all market sizing is 2024.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Executive summary

Utilities are replacing mechanical water meters with connected devices to reduce non-revenue water, automate billing and detect leaks earlier. The market is moving from walk-by automated meter reading (AMR) towards fixed-network advanced metering infrastructure (AMI), which shifts value from hardware towards software and services.

Asia-Pacific is the largest regional market, driven by national water-efficiency programmes and rapid urban network expansion. North America and Europe follow, with growth concentrated in replacement cycles and regulatory leakage targets.

Headline market size, growth rate and regional share are reported in Section 4 (page 8).

## 1. Scope and definitions

The market covers three components:

- Hardware: AMR and AMI water meters (mechanical with encoder, ultrasonic and electromagnetic), communication modules and data concentrators.
- Software: meter data management (MDM), leak analytics, billing integration and customer portals.
- Services: installation, network deployment, managed services and maintenance contracts.

Market value is measured as end-user revenue in US dollars at constant 2024 exchange rates. Regions covered: North America, Europe, Asia-Pacific (APAC), Latin America, and Middle East and Africa.

## 3. Market drivers

Non-revenue water remains the strongest driver: utilities in many emerging markets lose a large part of treated water to leaks and billing errors. Smart meters allow district-level water balance and near real-time leak alarms.

Regulation is a second driver. Several governments now set leakage reduction targets or require interval data for tariff reform. Falling costs of narrowband IoT (NB-IoT) and LoRaWAN connectivity reduce the per-endpoint cost of AMI networks.

## Restraints and challenges

High upfront cost and long utility procurement cycles slow adoption, particularly for smaller municipal utilities. Battery life expectations of 15 years or more put pressure on device design. Data privacy concerns and cybersecurity requirements add to integration effort.

Interoperability is improving but remains uneven: many utilities still run proprietary head-end systems that make multi-vendor deployments difficult.

<!-- page: 7 -->
## Technology landscape

Ultrasonic meters are gaining share over mechanical meters because they have no moving parts and keep accuracy at low flow rates. On the network side, NB-IoT dominates new deployments in Asia-Pacific and Europe, while proprietary RF mesh networks remain common in North America.

Software differentiation is shifting to analytics: pressure management, leak localisation and consumption forecasting.

The global smart water meter market was valued at USD 4.21 billion in 2024 and is expected to grow at a CAGR of 11.2% from 2025 to 2030. Asia-Pacific accounted for 38% of 2024 revenue.

The market size includes hardware, software and services. The CAGR applies to the forecast period 2025-2030; CMR does not publish a separate growth rate for 2024 to 2025. Regional shares refer to the share of the total global market size.

## 5. Regional analysis

Asia-Pacific is the largest region. China continues large-scale replacement of mechanical meters, India's urban water missions fund metering in tier-1 and tier-2 cities, and Australian utilities are moving to digital metering for drought resilience.

North America growth is driven by AMI upgrades bundled with electricity and gas metering. In Europe, leakage targets in the United Kingdom, France and Spain support steady deployment.
